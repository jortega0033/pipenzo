Foundations

Tokens and primitives, fixed before any product screen. Stepped near-black surfaces do the layering instead of border lines; every control is a pill or a circle; type is either bold or regular. Component anatomy follows GitHub Primer where Primer has an opinion (banner, flash, toast, form control) and IBM Carbon where it doesn't (tile, carousel).

Risk-graded approval — LOW proceeds and is logged, MEDIUM blocks on a compact inline card, HIGH gets the full card plus an OS notification

The classifier is targeting ~95% LOW — routine edits inside the owned worktree, reads, greps — and that number is measured against the fixture corpus of real diffs and action envelopes, not assumed: if the corpus does not come out near 95%, the classifier is wrong. LOW actions get one muted line in the stream and nothing else. MEDIUM and HIGH both carry the pre-commitment record and a reject-reason field; only MEDIUM has Undo, only HIGH pings the OS immediately. Publishing is permanently HIGH — no auto-allow exists for it at any level. A cumulative strip per ticket adds up what has gone unreviewed — LOW +0.5, MEDIUM +1, a pre-commitment mismatch +3, HIGH +0 — and promotes the next MEDIUM to a full HIGH card once the score reaches 10.

Plan-review gate — Refine stops for a human before Implement starts, and the spec is what gets approved

Near-term post-MVP. Refine is read-only by construction, so when it finishes there is a spec and nothing else: no worktree, no branch, no run spent. This gate makes that pause explicit rather than passive — a blocking card at the Refine step, not a line in the stream. It reuses the stack-approval anatomy exactly, because it is the same kind of decision at the same point in the run: head, what it is, the evidence, a reason field, then the actions. The four things it shows are the four things Refine emits — acceptance criteria in EARS notation, an explicit out-of-scope list, files likely touched, and the diff-size estimate — and the estimate matters twice, because it is also what the diff-scope gate measures the real diff against at Review. PR-event reactions · ci-failed and merge-conflict, why they land in different lanes, and the third one that has no design yet Both are things GitHub tells pipenzo about an open PR after the push gate, and both come back to a person. They part company on whether there is already something to look at. ci-failed runs one classifiable fix attempt as a session.fork, so a fix commit is already sitting on the branch waiting on the push gate — that card belongs in Ready for review. merge-conflict has no fix yet and pipenzo will not invent one, because a rebase is a decision: the card parks in Needs human with the conflicting files named and one primary action.

Red is reserved for something that failed or is blocking. A branch that went stale while it waited is neither, so merge-conflict reads amber — the same register as pipenzo:interrupted and "syncing slowly". The rebased branch is never force-pushed on its own; it re-enters the same human push gate, exactly like the ci-failed fix. The third reaction has no UI, and this section is two of three rather than a complete set. A card drawn before that is answered would be inventing the answer.
